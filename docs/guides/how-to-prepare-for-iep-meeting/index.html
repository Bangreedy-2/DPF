How to Prepare for an IEP Meeting: A 2-Week Countdown

A parent's countdown checklist for IEP meetings, with questions to ask and calm phrases for a rushed meeting.

IEP meetings move fast, the room is full of professionals, and it is easy to walk out having agreed to things you did not fully follow. Preparation changes that. This two-week countdown is for parents in the US. It is an organizing guide, not legal advice, and timelines vary by state.

Two weeks before

  • Ask in writing for the documents the team will discuss: the draft IEP, new evaluation reports and progress reports. Ask to receive them a few days before the meeting so you can read them at home.
  • Confirm who will attend, and tell the school if you are bringing someone (a partner, a friend, an advocate).
  • Re-read the current IEP. Highlight each goal and each service with its minutes.

One week before

  • Check progress on every goal. For each one, ask: is there data, and is it moving? A goal with no measurable progress for two reporting periods is worth raising.
  • Check services. Is your child getting the speech, OT or other minutes the IEP promises? Note any sessions you know were missed.
  • Collect your own evidence: work samples, teacher emails, notes on homework time, anything from outside providers.
  • Ask your child what is hard, what helps and what they wish their teachers knew.

Three days before

  • Write your top three concerns. If the meeting runs short, these are the things that must be discussed.
  • Write your questions. Useful ones include:
    • "How will this goal be measured, and how often will I see the data?"
    • "What does this accommodation look like in the classroom on a normal day?"
    • "If a service session is missed, how is it made up?"
    • "What would you expect to see by the next progress report?"
  • Read the draft IEP if you have it, and mark anything you do not understand or do not agree with.

On the day

  • Bring your binder, your top three concerns, your questions and a pen.
  • Start by saying what is going well, then name your three concerns.
  • Ask for plain English whenever an acronym goes past you.
  • Write down each decision, who is responsible and by when.

Calm phrases for a rushed meeting

  • "Can we pause there? I'd like to understand that before we move on."
  • "Can you show me the data behind that?"
  • "I'd like that noted in the meeting notes, please."
  • "I'd like to take the document home and read it before I sign."

You are generally not required to sign at the table. Taking the IEP home to review is a normal request. Rules on consent differ by state, so check your state's parent rights booklet.

Within two days after

  • Send a short follow-up email that lists what was agreed, who will do it and by when. It creates a written record.
  • Put the dates in your calendar: next progress report, next review, any evaluation deadlines.
  • File the new IEP and notes while they are fresh.

This is an organizing guide, not legal advice. For legal questions, contact your state's Parent Training and Information Center, an advocate or a special education attorney.
