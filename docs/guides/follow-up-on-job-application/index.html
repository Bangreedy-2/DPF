How to Follow Up on a Job Application (Timeline + 3 Email Templates)

When to follow up after applying for a job, who to email and three short templates you can copy.

Free printable: Free Job Application Follow-Up Kit. Download it free.

You applied, you heard nothing, and now you are wondering whether a follow-up looks keen or pushy. A short, polite follow-up is normal, and many hiring teams expect one. The hard part is timing it and then letting go. Here is a simple rule and three emails you can copy.

The 7 / 7 / close rule

  1. Day 7: send one short follow-up, about a week after you applied (or after the closing date, if the posting had one).
  2. Day 14: send one final check-in.
  3. Then close it. Mark the application as closed in your tracker and move your energy to the next one. If they reply later, it is a nice surprise.

Two messages is enough. A third rarely changes the outcome, and closing the loop yourself stops old applications from sitting in your head. Adjust the timing if the posting says otherwise: if it says "we will reply within three weeks", wait three weeks.

Who to send it to

  • The recruiter or hiring manager named in the posting, if there is one.
  • The person who sent your application confirmation.
  • If you only have a general careers address, use it and put the job title and reference number in the subject line.

Template 1: first follow-up (day 7)

Subject: Following up: [Job title] application

Hi [Name],

I applied for the [Job title] role on [date] and wanted to confirm my application reached you. I'm still very interested, especially in [one specific thing about the role or team].

I'm happy to send anything else that would help. Thank you for your time.

[Your name]
[Phone] · [LinkedIn or portfolio]

Template 2: final check-in (day 14)

Subject: Re: Following up: [Job title] application

Hi [Name],

I'm checking in one last time on the [Job title] role. If the position has been filled or the timing has changed, no problem at all. I'd be glad to be considered for similar roles in future.

Thanks again,
[Your name]

Template 3: thank-you after an interview

Subject: Thank you: [Job title] interview

Hi [Name],

Thank you for your time today. I enjoyed hearing about [specific project or challenge they mentioned], and it made me more interested in the role. [One sentence linking your experience to that challenge.]

Please let me know if I can share anything else.

[Your name]

Send the thank-you within a day of the interview. If they gave you a decision date, wait until two working days after it before you check in.

Four mistakes that cost replies

  • Following up the next day. Give it the full week.
  • Writing a second cover letter. Keep it under 100 words.
  • Not naming the job. Recruiters handle many roles at once. Put the title in the subject line.
  • Relying on memory. With 20 or more applications open, you will lose track of who is due a follow-up. Write the applied date down and let a tracker work out the rest.

Make the follow-ups automatic

The rule only works if you know which applications hit day 7 today. A spreadsheet can do that for you: type the date you applied, and a formula adds seven days and highlights the rows that need action.
