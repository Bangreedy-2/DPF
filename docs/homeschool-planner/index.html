Plan every child on one page. Never rewrite a week you fell behind on. Finish the year with your records already done.

Most homeschool planners are dated, made for one child, and stop at lesson plans. Then one sick week means re-dating everything, four kids means four planners, and June means rebuilding a year of attendance and hours from memory. This planner is the second, better draft.

What makes it different

  • Undated lesson tracks. Write each curriculum once as numbered lessons (180 per track). Each day, do the next one and shade the box. A sick day moves nothing, so there's nothing to rewrite.
  • Up to 4 kids on one page. The family week gives each child a colour and a row, plus a row for what you teach together. You see the whole day in one place.
  • Records as you go. A 180-day attendance grid, a weekly hours log (core and other subjects), work samples, field trips, tests and the paperwork you filed.
  • Your state, not a one-size form. A worksheet for the 7 things to look up about your own state's law: notice, days or hours, subjects, records, tests or review, teacher rules, high school.
  • Know early if a book won't fit the year. A catch-up page, and a tracker that shows a projected finish date for every curriculum and how many lessons a week gets you back on track.
  • Year end and high school. Year-end summary, progress report, portfolio checklist, 4-year course plan, transcript (with GPA in the tracker) and course descriptions.

What you get

  • 42-page printable planner in US Letter and A4 (print only the pages you need)

Inside the 5 tabs

Plan: my state's requirements, year at a glance, curriculum list, lesson tracks (example + blank), 12-week plan, catch-up page, monthly plan.
